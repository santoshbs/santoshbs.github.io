Far (2046–2051): For this occupation broadly: whether AI can perform its own physical maintenance remains an unresolvable uncertainty. One plausible outcome involves technicians managing fleets of robots that autonomously perform basic troubleshooting and component swaps [3]. A rival outcome suggests that strict safety and liability regimes will mandate human-in-the-loop verification for all critical calibrations [2]. The proliferation of diverse forms—including humanoids [4][8] and quadrupeds [7]—increases the likelihood that maintenance requires specialized human knowledge of different physical architectures. The task of making repairs to robots, specifically the replacement of heavy structural components or large actuators, resists full automation due to the high cost of developing specialized repair hardware for all possible robot forms [2].

Hypothesis A: In the far term, technicians may transition into supervisory roles managing autonomous maintenance loops for physical AI fleets.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, liability and safety regimes may require human technicians to certify every physical re-calibration to ensure safety standards are met.
