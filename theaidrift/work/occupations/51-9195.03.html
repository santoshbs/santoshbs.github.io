Far (2046–2051): For workers in these manufacturing contexts, the long-term outcome depends on whether safety mandates drive a transition toward full enclosure. One plausible outcome is that the physical act of carving rough designs freehand [10] is relegated to highly controlled, enclosed environments to eliminate human exposure to silica [1]. A rival outcome is that the high cost of safety infrastructure leads to a consolidation of the manufacturing base, where only large-scale operations can afford the required ventilation and filtration systems. It remains unknown whether the cost of these safety systems will eventually outweigh the economic value of manual stone carving in manufacturing. Even at this horizon, the sense of artistry required to carve designs in full or bas relief [9] is likely to resist full automation due to the unpredictable nature of natural stone veins.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, the high overhead of safety compliance may reduce the number of viable manufacturing shops, concentrating the occupation in a few highly capitalized firms.
