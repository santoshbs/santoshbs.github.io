Hypothesis: In the near term, penetration testers will integrate autonomous vulnerability discovery and AI-specific attack vectors into their workflows, while specialists in cloud and automotive sectors adopt domain-specific agents for simulation.

Mid (2036–2041): For penetration testers broadly: if current trajectories in autonomous vulnerability mapping and discovery hold, the task to identify security system weaknesses may shift from manual discovery to the verification and auditing of AI-generated attack paths [10][18]. Extrapolating from trends in red team workflow automation, the task to gather cyber intelligence to identify vulnerabilities could be partially managed by orchestration platforms that standardize the reconnaissance process [3]. In heavily regulated financial environments, the role may shift toward validating the risk briefs provided by regulators regarding AI-driven cyber risks [28]. However, the task to write audit reports to communicate technical and procedural findings persists as a human-led activity because the attribution of risk and alignment with organizational business logic require human accountability [2].

Hypothesis: In the mid term, technical exploitation may shift toward the auditing of autonomous AI-led attack chains, while formal reporting and regulatory validation remain human-centric due to accountability constraints.

Far (2046–2051): For penetration testers broadly: the long-term status of the task to test the security of systems depends on whether AI agents achieve a stable capacity to discover novel vulnerabilities without human-defined heuristics [4][10]. It remains unknown if the acceleration of AI-driven discovery will lead to real-time automated remediation that effectively closes the window for manual penetration testing [17]. The task to discuss security solutions with information technology teams or management resists change because negotiating risk appetite and organizational priorities requires human-centric social navigation [2].

Hypothesis A: In the far term, autonomous agents handle the majority of technical vulnerability discovery and exploitation, shifting the human role to strategic risk orchestration.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, the proliferation of autonomous, real-time patching systems renders traditional penetration testing obsolete, shifting the occupation toward the design of defensive AI guardrails.
