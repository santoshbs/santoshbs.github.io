Far (2046–2051): If robotic sensing fleets become an industry standard, technicians broadly may transition from active data collection to the orchestration of autonomous systems [1]. A rival outcome exists where the integration of physical AI [2] directly into machinery software removes the need for a separate technician to program equipment, potentially consolidating the role into general agricultural engineering. It remains unknown whether the labor market will preserve a distinct technician class or absorb these tasks into farm management roles. The physical requirement to calibrate hardware in unconditioned outdoor environments [3] will likely resist total automation due to equipment wear and unpredictable terrain.
