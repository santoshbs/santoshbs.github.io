Near (2026–2031): For this occupation broadly, the integration of terminal-based AI agents alters the task of writing, analyzing, reviewing, and rewriting programs by shifting the workflow from manual code entry to agent-led generation [1][4][7]. Multi-model verification features, such as cross-model review, modify the mechanism for correcting errors by providing automated second opinions to validate logical consistency [5]. For a subset of workers in logistics, the use of specialized agents may automate the maintenance of computer programs that handle specific jobs, such as managing rate complexity [2]. In organizations adopting AI-driven development platforms, these tools scale the ability to write and update programs across enterprise infrastructures [6]. For entry-level programmers, the automation of basic authorship tasks may reduce the demand for those performing routine coding, potentially narrowing the traditional career entry point [3]. However, the emergence of “vibe coding”—where developers rely on high-level prompts without manual verification—introduces risks that complicate the task of correcting errors when AI-generated logic fails silently [8]. The task of consulting with managerial, engineering, and technical personnel to clarify program intent is unlikely to change, as agent-led generation still requires high-fidelity human specifications to avoid logical drift [2][5].

Hypothesis: In the near term, computer programmers may transition from manual code authorship to the supervision of terminal-based agents, while the entry point for junior-level roles may narrow.

Mid (2036–2041): For this occupation broadly, if the cost of proprietary agents is offset by open-source alternatives [1], autonomous agents may become the primary mechanism for correcting errors and rechecking programs through iterative, self-correcting loops [5]. Extrapolating from specialized deployments in logistics [2] and the automation of routine scripts [9], the translation of complex business logic into executable code could reduce the manual effort required for the revision, repair, or expansion of existing programs [6]. The requirement to compile and write documentation of program development will likely persist as a human-led activity to ensure system auditability in regulated environments [5]. The task of investigating whether networks, workstations, or peripheral equipment are responding to program instructions is unlikely to change, as symbolic agents lack the physical observability required for hardware-level troubleshooting.

Hypothesis: In the mid term, the occupation may shift toward the validation and orchestration of AI-generated logic, with routine maintenance becoming an automated process across various organizational settings.

Far (2046–2051): For this occupation broadly, the long-term trajectory depends on whether AI-generated code remains legible to human overseers. One plausible outcome is that the task of preparing detailed workflow charts and diagrams is fully replaced by the management of autonomous agents that handle the entire lifecycle of maintaining computer programs [1][4]. A competing outcome is that the accumulation of opaque, AI-generated legacy code creates a “complexity trap,” where the loss of human-authored mental models forces a return to manual revision and repair of systems that agents cannot safely modify without introducing regressions [3][8]. It remains unknown whether professional liability regimes will mandate a human author of record for all production code, which would constrain the degree of agent autonomy. The application of symbolic logic to resolve deep structural contradictions in software architecture is likely to resist full automation even at this horizon.

Hypothesis A: In the far term, the role may evolve into a high-level system orchestrator, where the programmer manages a fleet of autonomous agents handling the full software lifecycle.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, the accumulation of unmanageable AI-generated legacy code may force a resurgence in manual programming to recover system stability and transparency.
