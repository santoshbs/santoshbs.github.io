The need to travel over photographed areas to verify relevant features is likely to persist to ensure ground-truth accuracy where digital models are outdated or imprecise [2].

Hypothesis: In the mid term, the occupational focus in advanced digital-twin regions may shift toward the maintenance and synchronization of live spatial data layers.

Far (2046–2051): For a subset of workers in regions with advanced open-access spatial data, the occupation may diverge based on the nature of data ownership. In one scenario, the role of the photogrammetrist may merge with GIS data science as the manual preparation of 3D optical models becomes a legacy task entirely superseded by automated government pipelines [1][2]. A rival outcome could occur if private proprietary data silos outpace public models, requiring workers to maintain bespoke, high-resolution data collection pipelines for private corporate assets [2]. The necessity of inspecting final compositions to ensure completeness and accuracy will resist change due to the catastrophic error cost of spatial inaccuracies in infrastructure and legal mapping [2].
