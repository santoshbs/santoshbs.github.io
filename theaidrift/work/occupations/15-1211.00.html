Computer Systems Analysts

O*NET Code: 15-1211.00

Created: 2026-04-04 (Model: qwen3.5:27b) Last revised: 2026-04-17 (Model: gemma4:31b-it-q8_0)

Near (2026–2031): For the occupation broadly, analysts are increasingly performing AI mapping to link technical AI capabilities to specific business requirements to drive revenue [2]. In organizations adopting agentic workflows, the task of analyzing information processing needs and planning computer systems shifts from designing static, rules-based automation to implementing agent-first process redesign [4]. For analysts in public sector environments, this involves configuring AI integrations to operate within specific security and governance constraints [6]. For a subset of workers in smart manufacturing, the task of coordinating and linking computer systems now includes integrating Internet of Things (IoT) data with customer relationship management (CRM) platforms [1]. In corporate administrative settings, analysts may assess the usefulness of pre-developed application packages to implement AI-powered employee onboarding agents [3]. In organizations using cloud-based AI platforms, analysts use new tools to attribute inference costs to specific workloads, which alters how they prepare cost-benefit and return-on-investment analyses [5]. The task of troubleshooting program and system malfunctions is unlikely to change significantly because the initial diagnostic phase for system failures remains a manual, technical process [7].

Hypothesis: In the near term, analysts broadly will shift toward mapping AI capabilities to business outcomes and redesigning processes for autonomous agents, while subsegments in manufacturing, government, and cloud operations integrate specialized agentic and cost-tracking tools.

Mid (2036–2041): For the occupation broadly, if current trajectories in agent-first design hold, analysts may transition from initial mapping to the continuous optimization of dynamic systems that adapt in real time [4]. Extrapolating from current integration patterns in smart manufacturing, analysts in that subsegment could expand or modify systems to enable autonomous, closed-loop predictive maintenance [1]. In administrative subsegments, the focus may shift from building single-purpose agents to managing ecosystems of interconnected lifecycle agents [3]. For analysts in cloud-native environments, the ability to track granular inference costs could evolve from retrospective analysis to real-time resource reallocation [5]. The task of consulting with management to ensure agreement on system principles will persist because the governance of autonomous agent behavior requires human negotiation regarding organizational risk [7].

Hypothesis: In the mid term, analysts may transition from designing static system architectures to managing the evolutionary behavior and economic efficiency of autonomous agent ecosystems.

Far (2046–2051): Scope is limited to organizations where agentic orchestration and high-frequency data integration are central to the business model. One plausible outcome is that analysts continue to define the goals of the system and devise flow charts to manage the high-level orchestration of multi-agent systems, as business logic remains too heterogeneous for total automation [4]. A rival outcome exists if AI mapping tools evolve to autonomously bridge the gap between business needs and technical implementation, reducing the analyst’s role in the initial design phase [2]. It remains unknown whether the mapping function will persist as a distinct human activity or be absorbed into a software self-configuration layer. The task of interviewing or surveying workers to determine how information is processed will resist change because capturing tacit, uncodified human expertise requires social interaction that agents cannot replicate [7].

Hypothesis A: In the far term, analysts act as high-level orchestrators of multi-agent systems, managing the interface between heterogeneous business logic and autonomous execution.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, autonomous mapping and configuration tools absorb the primary design and implementation tasks of the analyst.
