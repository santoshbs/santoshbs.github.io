Information Security Engineers

Near (2026–2031): For information security engineers broadly, the proliferation of residential proxies renders IP-based reputation systems insufficient, shifting the task of developing or installing software to protect sensitive information toward identity-centric verification and zero-trust architectures [5]. To identify security system weaknesses, engineers must implement kernel-level protections to counter “bring your own vulnerable driver” (BYOVD) techniques used by ransomware to disable endpoint detection and response (EDR) tools [6][36]. The emergence of non-human identities and agentic memory attacks, which can leak data across sessions, alters the task of implementing solutions to information security problems by requiring the design of isolation boundaries and the deployment of agent governance toolkits [14][18][31]. The discovery of vulnerabilities such as GrafanaGhost, where AI injection enables data theft, and the rise of browser extensions as AI consumption channels shift the task of scanning networks to identify vulnerabilities toward auditing prompt-injection vectors and third-party integration points [13][27]. To match the speed of agentic attacks, the development of response and recovery strategies is shifting from reactive patching toward proactive architectural defense [8][11]. For engineers in critical infrastructure settings, targeted attacks on PLC and SCADA systems shift the task of identifying security system weaknesses toward the hardening of operational technology [12][16][28]. The manual verification of physical hardware seals on air-gapped servers is unlikely to change, as these protocols rely on physical tamper-evidence rather than digital signals.

Hypothesis: In the near term, information security engineers will transition from IP-based perimeter defenses toward identity-centric controls and kernel-level hardening, while a subset of workers implements specialized governance for non-human AI identities.

Mid (2036–2041): For information security engineers broadly, if high-capability cybersecurity models move from limited preview to operational deployment, the task of identifying security system weaknesses may shift from manual penetration tests toward overseeing automated vulnerability discovery and remediation loops [9][10][19][20][23][34][35][40]. Extrapolating from current agentic attack speeds, the task of coordinating monitoring of networks for security breaches may shift toward the orchestration of autonomous defense agents that anticipate attack vectors before they are deployed [11][15]. For a subset of workers managing high-sensitivity data, the emergence of quantum computing threats may shift the task of developing software to protect sensitive information toward the implementation of post-quantum cryptographic standards [26]. The requirement for human sign-off on high-impact policy changes, such as the isolation of critical infrastructure segments during a suspected breach, persists because the catastrophic error cost of a security policy failure requires human accountability.

Hypothesis: In the mid term, the occupation may shift from manual monitoring and testing toward the orchestration of autonomous predictive defense systems, provided that high-capability AI models are integrated into operational workflows.

Far (2046–2051): For a subset of workers managing autonomous systems, the long-term trajectory may lead to the adoption of mission-scale zero-trust platforms to govern non-human identities and autonomous agents [24]. A primary uncertainty remains whether regulatory regimes will mandate human-in-the-loop overrides for all autonomous security actions or permit fully automated defensive responses. One rival outcome is that the increasing complexity of AI-driven attacks creates a “red queen” race, where the task of conducting investigations of information security breaches becomes entirely dependent on specialized forensic AI, reducing the human role to auditing the AI’s reasoning. The basic requirement for establishing a chain of custody for legal evidence during a forensic investigation resists change, as it is a legal requirement rather than a technical one.

Hypothesis A: In the far term, security engineering for autonomous systems transitions to the management of mission-scale zero-trust governance platforms.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, regulatory mandates for human accountability restrict autonomous security actions, maintaining the human as the primary decision-maker in response and recovery strategies.
