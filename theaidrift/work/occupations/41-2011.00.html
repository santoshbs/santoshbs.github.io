Near (2026–2031): In organizations deploying specialized AI assistants, the scope of the cashier’s role may narrow as automated platforms take over specific informational tasks. Because platforms can now handle inquiries regarding POS operations, maintenance, and payments [1], the frequency with which workers must provide information on procedures or policies (ONET Task 8) and resolve customer complaints (ONET Task 4) may decrease. This shift moves the cashier from the primary point of procedural resolution to a secondary supervisor of the AI’s output. However, tasks requiring tactile manipulation, such as bagging, boxing, or wrapping merchandise (O*NET Task 26), are unlikely to change in these settings because AI assistants lack the physical embodiment required for material handling.

Hypothesis: In the near term, a subset of cashiers in organizations using AI assistants may experience a reduction in the cognitive load associated with processing routine procedural inquiries.

Mid (2036–2041): For cashiers in small-format retail environments, if current trajectories hold, the use of AI assistants for POS-related troubleshooting may migrate from individual early-adopting organizations to a broader class of similar retail settings [1]. This could lead to a further atrophy of the need for cashiers to answer customers’ questions (ONET Task 8) as automated systems standardize the delivery of operational information. Despite this, the requirement to maintain clean and orderly checkout areas (ONET Task 12) persists, as AI software cannot perform the physical cleaning duties required for site hygiene.

Hypothesis: In the mid term, the role of cashiers in small-format retail may shift further toward physical facility maintenance and complex transaction oversight if AI assistants standardize procedural support.

Far (2046–2051): In the subsegment of retail environments utilizing integrated AI assistants, the trajectory of the occupation remains uncertain due to a tension between automation and the error costs of financial disputes. One plausible outcome is that the task of resolving complaints (ONET Task 4) is almost entirely automated, leaving the human worker to focus primarily on physical security and inventory. A rival outcome is that the high friction associated with payment errors [1] creates a renewed demand for human-centric validation for all non-standard transactions. The task of greeting customers entering establishments (ONET Task 2) may resist change even at this horizon, provided that social norms continue to prioritize human interaction during the initial point of entry in physical commerce.

Hypothesis A: In the far term, cashiers in AI-integrated environments may evolve into physical site stewards whose primary function is security and inventory management.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, cashiers may see a resurgence in the importance of social perceptiveness as AI assistants handle all routine tasks, leaving only high-emotion conflict resolution to the human worker.
