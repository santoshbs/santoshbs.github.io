Near (2026–2031): For patient representatives broadly, the deployment of hospital-branded AI chatbots may shift the initial execution of explaining policies, procedures, or services [1][2] and directing patient inquiries [1] from human agents to automated interfaces. Because hospitals are implementing these tools to capture patient traffic and handle routine triage [2], representatives may move into a supervisory role where they audit AI-generated responses for accuracy and handle escalations. This mechanism transitions the worker from a primary information provider to an exception handler for complex or non-standard inquiries. The task of interviewing patients to identify problems relating to care [O*NET] is unlikely to change significantly in this window, as it requires high levels of social perceptiveness to identify nuanced patient distress that current chatbot deployments cannot detect.

Hypothesis: In the near term, patient representatives will transition from primary responders to auditors and exception handlers for AI-mediated patient communications.

Mid (2036–2041): For the whole occupation, if current trajectories of proprietary hospital AI hold, the task of coordinating communication between patients, family members, and medical staff [ONET] may become a data-management function. Extrapolating from the shift toward AI-driven triage [2], representatives may move toward analyzing interaction logs to identify systemic bottlenecks in patient access rather than manually managing individual communication threads. The task of referring patients to appropriate health care services or resources [ONET] will likely persist as a human-mediated activity because navigating local, non-digitized community resources requires situational knowledge that is not readily captured in hospital datasets.

Hypothesis: In the mid term, the role may shift from individual case navigation toward systemic quality improvement based on the analysis of AI-patient interaction data.

Far (2046–2051): For the whole occupation, it remains unknown whether AI will manage the entirety of the administrative patient interface or if regulatory regimes will mandate a human advocate for patient rights and grievances. One plausible outcome is that the representative becomes a specialized advocate for high-complexity cases where AI fails to resolve emotional or ethical conflicts. A competing outcome is that the role is absorbed into general medical administration as AI handles nearly all policy communication and resource referral [1][2]. Despite these shifts, the provision of advocacy during acute medical crises will resist automation due to the structural requirement for human empathy and moral agency in healthcare delivery.

Hypothesis A: In the far term, the occupation may bifurcate into a high-complexity advocacy role for crisis management and a technical oversight role for AI health systems.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, the role may be largely absorbed into general medical administration as AI automates the majority of patient navigation and policy explanation.
