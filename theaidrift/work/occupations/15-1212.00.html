Near (2026–2031): For information security analysts broadly, because residential proxy networks enable attack traffic to bypass IP-reputation filters [3], the mechanism for regulating access to safeguard information shifts toward behavioral analysis and identity-based telemetry. Because AI-assisted supply chain attacks increasingly target open-source repositories [9] and malicious packages are distributed across npm, PyPI, and Go [33], the task of monitoring current reports of computer viruses shifts toward continuous provenance auditing of third-party dependencies [9][33]. For a subset of workers in organizations deploying autonomous agents, the process of regulating access to safeguard information now includes managing non-human identities and mitigating agentic memory attacks that persist across sessions [26][64]. For analysts in regions where critical infrastructure is targeted, monitoring security measures focuses on detecting disruptive attacks targeting industrial process controllers and PLC devices [24][28]. Training users and promoting security awareness is unlikely to change yet because industrialized social engineering continues to exploit fundamental human cognitive biases regardless of technical controls [10][16].

Hypothesis: In the near term, analysts broadly may transition toward behavioral detection and provenance auditing, while specific subsegments integrate specialized risk intelligence for agentic AI, non-human identities, or industrial control systems.

Mid (2036–2041): For information security analysts broadly, if the collapse of the zero-day timeline [30] and the rise of agentic attack speeds [22] persist, the task of performing risk assessments may pivot from monitoring mean time to detection toward closing the “post-alert gap” via automated remediation [60]. For a subset of workers in regulated environments, the task of documenting computer security policies may transition from reviewing probabilistic test results to using automated reasoning and formal verification to produce mathematically proven compliance results [79]. For workers in agentic settings, the integration of AI-native threat intelligence platforms [44] and agent governance toolkits [31][37] may shift the process of modifying computer security files to focus on the real-time governance of autonomous agent permissions and intent [1][36]. Extrapolating from quantum computing warnings, the mechanism of encrypting data transmissions may shift toward the deployment of post-quantum cryptographic standards to maintain confidentiality [43]. Reviewing violations of computer security procedures and discussing procedures with violators persists as a human-centric task because correcting human behavioral errors requires qualitative social judgment and interpersonal negotiation [10].

Hypothesis: In the mid term, analysts broadly may move toward auditing automated agent interactions and remediation speed, while the core of policy enforcement remains anchored in human social judgment.

Far (2046–2051): For information security analysts broadly, the scope of the occupation remains bounded by the persistence of an environment where AI-native models can discover vulnerabilities faster than human-managed systems can patch them [62]. The primary unknown is whether the defensive mechanism settles on full AI-orchestrated autonomy or a structural retreat to hardware-based constraints to limit attack surfaces. Because the cost of software-based error remains high in critical systems, certain components of the task to encrypt data transmissions may resist change by reverting to air-gapped or physical isolation protocols. One rival outcome involves the standardization of recursive AI-driven patching where the analyst’s role shifts entirely to the high-level orchestration of these competing automated systems. A competing outcome involves the failure of software-based trust, leading to a requirement for physical, human-verified identity and access controls for all sensitive data movements.

Hypothesis A: In the far term, analysts broadly may function as orchestrators of autonomous recursive defense systems that patch vulnerabilities in real-time.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, analysts broadly may shift toward managing physical isolation and hardware-based security boundaries as software-based defenses become computationally untenable.
