Near (2026–2031): For retail salespersons broadly, the availability of AI-driven digital interfaces may reduce the human associate’s role in basic information retrieval. Because customers can scan QR codes to receive instant answers about product specifications [1], the task of answering questions regarding merchandise may shift toward automated systems. This mechanism could similarly affect how workers describe merchandise and explain its use to customers [1]. Consequently, recommending merchandise based on customer needs may become a shared task between the worker and an AI advisor [1]. Tasks requiring physical presence and manual dexterity, such as cleaning shelves, counters, and tables, are unlikely to change as they lack a digital equivalent [2].

Hypothesis: In the near term, basic product information retrieval and initial needs assessment may shift from human associates to AI-driven digital interfaces in physical retail environments.

Mid (2036–2041): For retail salespersons broadly, if current trajectories hold, the occupation may transition from being the primary source of product knowledge to acting as a curator of AI-generated options. Extrapolating from the deployment of digital advisors [1], the task of recommending merchandise may evolve into validating AI suggestions for the customer. The human associate could focus more on the physical preparation of merchandise for purchase or rental, which remains a manual process [2]. The core requirement to watch for and recognize security risks is likely to persist unchanged because of the unpredictable nature of physical theft and the need for human judgment in conflict resolution [2].

Hypothesis: In the mid term, the occupation may move toward a hybrid model where AI handles data-driven recommendations while humans manage physical fulfillment and store security.

Far (2046–2051): For retail salespersons broadly, the long-term structure of the occupation depends on the potential integration of embodied robotics into the sales floor. One rival outcome is that high-value retail remains a human-centric experience to maintain a luxury signal, while budget retail becomes fully automated. Another uncertainty is whether AI can eventually perform the task of inventorying stock and requisitioning new stock without human oversight [2]. Even at this horizon, the task of handling complex returns and exchanges may resist full automation due to the nuanced social negotiation required to resolve customer disputes [2].

Hypothesis A: In the far term, the occupation may bifurcate into high-touch specialized consultation for luxury goods and low-touch logistics management for automated stores.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, widespread adoption of embodied robotics may automate both the information and the physical handling tasks, reducing the human role to a minimal supervisory presence.
