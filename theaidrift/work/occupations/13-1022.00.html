Simultaneously, the emergence of AI-to-AI commerce may shift the task of negotiating prices, discount terms, or transportation arrangements toward automated agents for some businesses [2]. Mid (2036–2041): For wholesale and retail buyers broadly, if current trajectories in agentic commerce hold, the task of negotiating prices and terms for low-variability goods may move from direct human execution to the oversight of AI-to-AI interactions [2]. Extrapolating from the integration of digital spend management, the task of monitoring and analyzing sales records to anticipate consumer buying patterns could become more tightly coupled with automated ordering triggers, reducing the latency between demand signals and procurement [1]. Despite these shifts, the task of collaborating with vendors to obtain or develop desired products is unlikely to change because product innovation requires relational trust and iterative physical sampling [2]. The human requirement for recommending mark-up or mark-down rates persists as these decisions must align with broader corporate branding and competitive positioning [2].

Hypothesis: In the mid term, procurement for standard commodities may move toward an exception-based management model where buyers oversee automated negotiation and ordering triggers.

Far (2046–2051): For wholesale and retail buyers broadly, the long-term trajectory depends on whether merchandise selection remains a qualitative strategic choice or becomes a data-driven optimization. One outcome involves the near-total automation of the ordering, negotiation, and payment lifecycle for standard goods, where the buyer’s role is limited to setting high-level constraints for AI agents [1][2]. A rival outcome suggests a resurgence in curated buying, where the human-led examination and selection of merchandise based on qualitative trends becomes a premium differentiator against algorithmically generated assortments [2]. A primary unknown remains the evolution of regulatory regimes regarding the legal agency of AI in binding contractual agreements. Even in highly automated scenarios, the physical inspection of high-value goods to determine yield or quality likely resists change due to the inherent sensory requirements of the task [2].
