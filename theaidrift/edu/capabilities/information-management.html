Near (2026–2031): For information management broadly, the processes of information search & discovery, information & data security management, and information collection management are shifting toward agentic delivery mechanisms. In information search & discovery, institutions are deploying hybrid RAG solutions [13], data-grounded chatbots for university knowledge bases [40], and multi-agent research systems [46], while the Model Context Protocol (MCP) is emerging as a technical standard for agentic tool integration and resource discovery [60][61][63][66][69]. This change in delivery is accompanied by a shift in educational substance: the rise of AI-driven SEO to influence model responses [8][49] and a pivot toward technical accuracy as a primary discovery signal [9] may necessitate the adoption of structured AI citation registries to maintain information provenance [15], as retrieval efficacy is constrained by a move away from simple chunking in RAG architectures [59]. Within information & data security management, the deployment of MCP servers has introduced vulnerabilities, including information disclosure [11] and prompt injection [19], driving the adoption of MCP-specific firewalls [36] and autonomous agent governance tools to mitigate “shadow AI” [3][14]. For institutions implementing frontier models, as demonstrated by the deployment of Google Gemini at Case Western Reserve University [1], security management incorporates domain-level filtering to restrict agent access [4] and the use of open-source runtime toolkits [31] or zero-trust security runtimes [29][57]. The emergence of models capable of identifying security vulnerabilities across operating systems and browsers [26][74] is accelerating the need for autonomous auditing as a core security process, while the ability of coding agents to exfiltrate environment variables and secrets [75] necessitates stricter runtime middleware [21] and token vaulting [18]. Furthermore, the rise of edge AI workloads is shifting security management from cloud-perimeter defenses to decentralized governance of edge workloads [72]. For institutions prioritizing verifiable data-training boundaries, security processes now include technical and contractually backed assurances that institutional data is not used for model training [71], while in Europe, these processes are constrained by regulatory scrutiny from the European Data Protection Board regarding AI agent data processing [51]. Information collection management is shifting toward “data activation” to unify fragmented sources [22] and the use of automated document extraction systems to reduce processing time for unstructured PDF archives [24], though regional progress varies; Japan has relaxed privacy laws to facilitate AI development [28], while the UK faces challenges in fueling AI with public data [30]. Records management is unlikely to change in this period because statutory retention schedules and legal archival mandates remain decoupled from AI-driven discovery tools.

Hypothesis: In the near term, information management will shift toward a hybrid RAG-based discovery model supported by citation registries, while security management moves from cloud-perimeter defense to zero-trust agent governance and autonomous vulnerability auditing to mitigate MCP-related risks and frontier model capabilities.

Mid (2036–2041): For information management broadly: if current trajectories in AI-driven vulnerability discovery hold, the process of information & data security management may shift from reactive patching to autonomous, real-time hardening as models become capable of identifying zero-day vulnerabilities across operating systems and browsers [26][54][64]. Extrapolating from the emergence of AI systems capable of multi-step infiltration and automated bug discovery [27][32], the mechanism of security management could evolve into a continuous adversarial loop where AI agents both identify and remediate vulnerabilities in real-time. In information search & discovery, if technical accuracy becomes the dominant SEO gold standard [9], the process of knowledge management may shift toward the curation of high-fidelity, machine-readable “truth sets” to ensure institutional authority in AI-generated responses. The process of information collection management may further automate the unification of disparate data streams via agentic “data activation” [22], potentially reducing the need for manual data mapping. Institutional governance structures regarding data ownership and legal copyright are unlikely to change rapidly during this period due to the slow pace of judicial precedent and legislative reform.

Hypothesis: In the mid term, information management will likely transition to an autonomous security posture of real-time hardening, while discovery processes shift from keyword-based retrieval to the management of high-fidelity truth sets for agentic consumption.

Far (2046–2051): For information management broadly: the long-term state of the capability depends on whether the security paradox—where AI both creates and solves vulnerabilities—reaches an equilibrium. If autonomous auditing and zero-trust runtimes [29][57] effectively neutralize AI-driven attacks [44], information & data security management may become a background utility with bounded autonomy, requiring only high-level policy oversight. Conversely, if the capacity for AI to find zero-day vulnerabilities [27][41] consistently outpaces the capacity for automated remediation, institutions may return to air-gapped or highly fragmented information silos to protect core intellectual property. The process of information search & discovery may evolve into an entirely synthetic interface where “search” is replaced by the direct synthesis of institutional knowledge, though the method for verifying the provenance of that synthesis remains an unresolvable uncertainty. The fundamental requirement for human-authorized records for legal and accreditation purposes is likely to persist even at this horizon, resisting full automation due to the necessity of social and legal accountability.

Hypothesis A: In the far term, information management becomes a seamless, self-healing ecosystem where autonomous agents manage discovery and security with minimal human intervention.

Hypothesis B (competing): In the far term, systemic security failures driven by frontier AI lead to a “re-siloing” of institutional information, where critical capabilities are isolated from networked AI to prevent total compromise.
